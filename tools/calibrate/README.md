# Calibrating the in situ kinetics

The in situ processes (`js/kinetics.js`) run on parameters that each carry a label:

- **literature**: a published constant, such as a radiolysis rate constant or a diffusion coefficient.
- **trend**: a placeholder chosen to reproduce a published trend.
- **calibrated**: fitted to measured in situ data. The fitting dataset is named next to the value.

A **calibration profile** (`temview-calibration/1`) holds the fitted values. The exhibit applies the published profile by default. Under **Calibration**, you can switch to the uncalibrated defaults or to your own profile. Your own profile turns the simulator into a digital twin of *your* instrument and specimen.

## What the published profile contains

`js/calibration-published.js` is generated by `node tools/calibrate/calibrate.mjs --published`. The full report, with every target, check and caveat, is in `published-report.json`.

| Dataset | Measured | Fitted | Not fitted (check) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Woehl, Evans, Arslan, Ristenpart, Browning, *ACS Nano* 6, 8599 (2012). Ag from 1 mM AgNO₃, liquid-cell STEM, 200 kV, 800 nm liquid. | Nothing nucleates below 0.5 e⁻/Å²/s (σ 0.1). The induction dose is 27.8 ± 7.8 e⁻/Å² whatever the beam settings. | `nuc_c_ag` 17.8 M (±32 %), `nuc_k_ag` 0.023 /s (±42 %) | At 6.7 e⁻/Å²/s the model gives 32 e⁻/Å² (measured 27.8 ± 7.8). |
| Horwath, Lehman-Chong, Vojvodic, Stach, arXiv:2209.15580 (2022). Au, R = 2.1 nm, on a SiNₓ heating chip, held ≥ 1 h at each temperature. | Eₐ = 1.2 eV for the size change (σ 0.15, assumed). Little change at 700 °C, rapid at 750 °C, so a 10 % change in 1 h happens at 998 ± 25 K. | `sinter_A` 670 Å/s (log-σ 4.9: correlated with Eₐ), `sinter_Ea` 1.2 eV (±13 %) | In 10 min at 800 K the model gives 0.01 Å. The old trend onset of ~750 K is gone. |

Caveats are written into the profile:

- **Nucleation, Woehl 2012.** The data are for silver, so the gold nucleation parameters stay at trend. The fitted nucleation constants are *effective*. c_n is not a solubility: it absorbs re-oxidation by OH·, loss of atoms from the irradiated region, and growth to the 5 nm detection limit.
- **Coarsening, Horwath 2022.** The gold rate was measured as evaporation in vacuum, where the particles shrink. The model applies the same thermally activated rate to coarsening in an inert gas. The oxygen factor stays at trend.

These parameters remain at trend because no suitable published series gives them as numbers:

- redox (SrTiO₃)
- electrochemistry (Butler–Volmer exchange rate and potential)
- the gold growth yield η
- the cell geometry around the beam (beam_r, chip_area, flow_bypass)

Fit these to your own data.

## Calibrating to your own data

1. **Set up the experiment in the exhibit as you ran it.** Choose the holder, cell, gas or liquid, precursor, dose rate and bias. Add the timeline of changes. Then **Data → Save session**.
2. **Calibration → Template CSV.** This writes the observables that apply to these conditions, sampled from the model. Replace the values with your measurements, one row per frame or time point:

   ```
   t_s,T_K,radius_A,radius_A_sd
   0,1003.2,21.0,0.3
   120,1012.8,21.1,0.3
   ```

   - **Time.** `t_s` is required.
   - **Input columns.** `T_K`, `lcRate`, `gcP` and `bsV` are inputs when present: the model runs at your measured temperature, dose rate, pressure or voltage instead of the setpoint.
   - **Fitted columns.** Every other known observable column is fitted.
   - **Uncertainties.** `<column>_sd` gives σ per point. Without it, σ is 5 % of the column's range.
3. **Calibration → Fit my series….** Name the parameters to fit, or leave the field empty to use the suggestions:

   | Measured | Parameters suggested |
   |---|---|
   | radius under heating | `sinter_A, sinter_Ea` |
   | ξ | `redox_A, redox_Ea` |
   | radius under bias in liquid | `bv_i0, bv_Eeq` |
   | radius in liquid | `Au_eta` |
   | reservoir H₂ or bubbles | `chip_area` |

   The fit builds on the profile in use. Parameters you do not fit keep their published or default values. The plot shows your points against the calibrated model, with the reduced χ².
4. **Use it.** The profile is applied as **Mine** and saved inside the session, so the twin reopens calibrated. **Save my profile** writes it as a file. Load it elsewhere with **Load profile…**.

The same fit runs headless:

```bash
node tools/calibrate/calibrate.mjs --session my-session.json --series my-series.csv --fit sinter_A,sinter_Ea --name "Au/SiNx, our heating chip" --out my-calibration.json
```

`--targets targets.json` adds scalar results, for example a nucleation threshold or an onset temperature measured in your own experiments:

```json
[{ "model": "nuc_dose", "at": { "lcRate": 2 }, "value": 30, "sigma": 5, "unit": "e⁻/Å²" },
 { "model": "size_change_T", "frac": 0.1, "hold": 3600, "value": 1010, "sigma": 20, "unit": "K" }]
```

Target models:

| Model | Gives | Extra fields |
|---|---|---|
| `param` | a parameter's value | `key` |
| `nuc_threshold` | the nucleation threshold dose rate | |
| `nuc_dose` | the induction dose | `at` |
| `nuc_time` | the induction time | `at` |
| `size_change` | the size change | `T`, `hold` |
| `size_change_T` | the temperature for a size change | `frac`, `hold` |
| `first_bubble_s` | the time to the first bubble | `tMax` |
| `nucleated_s` | the nucleation time | `tMax` |

## How the fit works

The fit is in `js/insitu-cal.js`.

- **Objective.** It minimises χ² over the chosen parameters. Rates and amounts are fitted on a log scale; energies and potentials on a linear scale.
- **Search.** Nelder–Mead runs from a grid of starts, then a polishing restart.
- **Uncertainties.** They come from the Jacobian at the optimum, cov = (JᵀJ)⁻¹. They are relative in the profile.
- **Speed.** Series are compared using a process model that steps the same functions as the simulator (liquid.js, gas.js, bias.js) without forming images. `tools/insitu-cal-check.mjs` checks it against the full simulator.
- **Test.** The same check fits a series made with known parameters back to them.
