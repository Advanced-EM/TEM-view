How small is it?

How does it work?

🔦

Electrons instead of light

A normal microscope uses light. Light waves are too big to show atoms. Electrons behave like waves that are about 100,000 times shorter, so they can show much smaller things.

🧲

Magnets are the lenses

Glass can't bend electrons, but magnets can. The microscope has coils of wire that make magnetic lenses: one for brightness, one for focus and one for zoom.

🫧

Thinner than a bubble

Electrons have to shoot straight through the sample, so it must be super thin. Thick parts stop more electrons and look dark. Real pictures are grey: scientists add colors to make them easier to see.

🔥

Don't cook it!

A very bright beam can damage soft samples like pollen and bacteria. Scientists use as little beam as they can. Try it: turn BRIGHT all the way up and watch.

For teachers and parents

Electron Explorer is the K-12 companion of Seeing with Electrons, a physics-based electron microscope simulator. It keeps three controls (focus, zoom, brightness), a joystick and a camera, and adds missions and a sticker book. Real microscopists use the same steps: turn on the beam, find the sample, focus, then take a picture without damaging it.

  • Grades K-2: turn on Read aloud; play the first three missions together; compare the sticker colors with "real" grey.
  • Grades 3-5: use the How small is it? ladder to put a hair, a cell, a virus and an atom in order. Links to 5-PS1-1 (matter is made of particles too small to be seen).
  • Grades 6-8: cells and viruses (MS-LS1-1); graphene as a structure built from atoms (MS-PS1-1); the crosscutting concept Scale, proportion and quantity.
  • Grades 9-12: why focus is harder at high zoom, why brightness costs damage, and why the "real" picture is grey. Then move on to the full simulator.

At a booth or in a museum, add ?kiosk to the address: the exhibit starts over after three minutes without anyone touching it. It runs with a mouse, a touch screen, a keyboard, a game controller or the TEM-view Console. The samples are drawn to scale from simple shapes. They are not real recorded images.
