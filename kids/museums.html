Electron Explorer in your museum

A cartoon electron microscope for visitors aged 5 and up: they turn on the beam, focus, zoom from a pollen grain to single atoms, complete missions with Zip the electron, and take home a sticker of what they saw. Five languages with recorded voices; touch screen, keyboard, game controller or the TEM-view Console.

Using it

Electron Explorer is free to use online for education and outreach, at this address. If you would like to install it in a museum, science center or exhibition, or to pilot it with your visitors, please get in touch: open an issue on GitHub.

Set up a kiosk

  1. Open the exhibit in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge with ?kiosk at the end of the address, and optionally a starting language: 
    https://advanced-em.github.io/TEM-view/kids/?kiosk&lang=es
  2. Start the browser full screen, with printing that doesn't ask (for stickers) and without pinch zoom: 
    # Windows (in a shortcut, or the Startup folder)
"C:\Program Files\Google\Chrome\Application\chrome.exe" --kiosk --kiosk-printing --disable-pinch --overscroll-history-navigation=0 --noerrdialogs --disable-features=Translate "https://advanced-em.github.io/TEM-view/kids/?kiosk"

# macOS
open -a "Google Chrome" --args --kiosk --kiosk-printing --disable-pinch --overscroll-history-navigation=0 --noerrdialogs "https://advanced-em.github.io/TEM-view/kids/?kiosk"

# Linux
google-chrome --kiosk --kiosk-printing --disable-pinch --overscroll-history-navigation=0 --noerrdialogs "https://advanced-em.github.io/TEM-view/kids/?kiosk"
  3. Open the staff menu (below), change the PIN, and choose the languages, voice, volume and take-home options.
  4. Press Save everything for offline use in the staff menu while the kiosk is online.

In kiosk mode the exhibit hides links that leave it, the teacher notes and the footer, and blocks right-click, text selection and page zoom. When nobody has touched it for the reset time (3 minutes by default), it starts over behind an attract screen that invites visitors in, with a button for each language.

Running offline

The exhibit, its fonts and Zip's voice are kept in the browser after Save everything for offline use, so the kiosk keeps working if the network drops. The QR code for take-home stickers needs the visitor's phone to be online, not the kiosk.

For a kiosk that is never online, copy the TEM-view folder to the computer and serve it locally, for example with Python, then open http://localhost:8000/kids/?kiosk:

cd TEM-view
python3 -m http.server 8000

The staff menu

Press and hold the Zip logo at the top left for 3 seconds (or press Ctrl+Alt+S), then enter the PIN on the keypad. The default PIN is 1933; change it on the first day. Settings are kept in that browser.

Take-home stickers

After each picture, visitors see Print my sticker and Take it home.

  • Printed sticker. Set the printer as the computer's default, start Chrome with --kiosk-printing so it prints without a dialog, and choose the paper in the staff menu: 2-inch round labels (for example on a Brother QL or DYMO label printer), a 58 mm receipt roll (thermal receipt printer), or 4 × 6 inch photo paper. Limit the stickers per visitor to control paper use.
  • QR code. A parent scans it with a phone; it opens a page that redraws the picture and lets them save or share it. The code holds only the picture's settings (sample, position, zoom, focus, brightness, colors, stars and language): no name, time, place or photo. If you add your own sample packs, host a copy of the exhibit online with them and put its sticker page address in the staff menu.

Visitor numbers

In kiosk mode the exhibit counts, for each day: visits, time spent (average and in bands from under 30 seconds to over 10 minutes), missions completed, visitors who finished every mission, pictures, stickers printed, QR codes shown, languages, samples used, and use of the accessibility options. A visit starts when someone touches the attract screen and ends when the exhibit starts over.

Privacy. The numbers are totals kept only on that computer, in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere; there are no cookies, identifiers, names, photos or recordings. Online (not in kiosk mode) Electron Explorer counts nothing. This is designed to meet children's privacy rules such as COPPA and the GDPR, but check with your own privacy officer.

Accessibility

Built in: Zip's speech bubble works as captions for every spoken line; Easier to see offers high contrast, bigger text and calm motion (staff can make them the default); every control works from a keyboard, a game controller or the Console, as well as by touch; knobs have − and + buttons for visitors who cannot turn or drag; nothing has a time limit except the idle reset; and the page respects the system's reduced-motion setting.

For the installation, follow the Smithsonian Guidelines for Accessible Exhibition Design and your local accessibility standards (in the US, the 2010 ADA Standards):

  • Put the touch screen and any Console within a forward reach of 15 to 48 inches (380 to 1220 mm) from the floor, and angle the screen so seated visitors and children can see it.
  • Leave knee space under the kiosk (about 27 inches, 685 mm, high and 30 inches, 760 mm, wide) so a wheelchair user can pull up.
  • Offer headphones with a volume limit or a directional speaker, and a printed label with the main instructions near the screen.
  • Avoid glare on the screen and keep the light even.

Languages and voices

The exhibit speaks English, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French and German. Each has a girl's and a boy's voice for Zip, recorded with ElevenLabs; a line without a recording is read by the computer's own voice. The texts are in kids/lines.js and kids/lang/, and tools/kids-i18n-check.mjs lists anything untranslated. Another language can be added the same way.

Your own samples

A sample pack adds a sample without code: a folder in kids/packs/ with a pack.json and its images (SVG, PNG or JPEG, for instance real micrographs from your collection at three zooms), plus an entry in kids/packs/index.json. A pack can bring its own descriptions, "What's this?" answers and a mission, in each language. The example pack is a diatom, from the whole glass shell down to its 40-nanometre pores. The format is in kids/packs/README.md.

Updates

A kiosk that loads the online address gets updates on its own the next time it starts online. A local copy is updated by replacing the folder. Settings, PIN and visitor numbers are kept in the browser and survive updates; clearing the browser's data erases them, so export the visitor numbers first.
