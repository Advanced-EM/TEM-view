SOP-02 STEM alignmentScope and preparation

Scope and preparation

This procedure sets up a focused probe for atomic-resolution STEM on a microscope with a probe aberration corrector. It assumes the gun is aligned and the specimen is at the eucentric height (SOP-01, steps 1 and 3). Without a corrector, follow the same steps but skip step 3 and use a smaller convergence angle.

Before you start

  • SOP-01 steps 1 and 3 are complete: gun aligned, specimen at eucentric height.
  • The specimen has a thin amorphous area (support film or specimen edge) close to the area of interest.
  • The crystal you will image is tilted to the zone axis, or close to it.
  • The Ronchigram camera and the STEM detectors are available and their protection settings are on.

Terms used

The convergence semi-angle α is the half-angle of the cone of the probe, set by the condenser aperture. The Ronchigram is the shadow image of the specimen formed in the far field of a stationary probe. Aberrations are named as in most corrector software: C1 defocus, A1 two-fold astigmatism, B2 axial coma, A2 three-fold astigmatism, C3 spherical aberration, A3 four-fold astigmatism.

SOP-02 STEM alignmentSteps 1 and 2 of 5
Step 1

Probe setup

  1. Switch to STEM mode (probe mode) and select the condenser aperture for the convergence semi-angle you need.
  2. Select the spot size, and check the probe current on the screen or with a Faraday cup.
  3. Blank the beam or move to a thin area before continuing.
Step 2

Ronchigram: focus and centering

  1. Move a thin amorphous area under the probe, stop the scan, and view the Ronchigram on the camera.
  2. Away from focus, the Ronchigram is a magnified shadow image of the specimen (Fig. 6a, 6b). Change the probe focus (C1) toward focus: the shadow image magnification increases toward the center.
  3. Near focus, the center of the Ronchigram becomes a region of nearly uniform contrast. Set the focus so that this region is as large as possible.
  4. Center this region in the field of view with beam tilt (on some instruments, the Ronchigram center or coma-free alignment).
  5. Center the condenser aperture on the same point.

Result. Near focus, the uniform central region and the aperture edge are concentric.

Note. The two sides of focus can be told apart by the direction in which the shadow image moves when the probe is moved across the specimen. Overfocus and underfocus move it in opposite directions.

Step 3

Aberration correction

The corrector cancels the spherical aberration of the objective lens and the residual aberrations of the column. The goal is a Ronchigram whose uniform central region is at least as large as the condenser aperture.

  1. Keep the probe on the thin amorphous area, slightly away from focus as the corrector software requires.
  2. Run the aberration measurement in the corrector software (Ronchigram analysis or a tilt tableau).
  3. Correct the low orders first: C1 and A1, then B2, then A2. Measure again after each correction.
  4. When the low orders are within the software's targets, correct C3, A3 and the higher orders the software reports.
  5. Repeat the measurement until the values are stable and within target.

Result. The measured aberrations are within the corrector targets, and the Ronchigram near focus is uniform out to at least the convergence semi-angle (Fig. 6d).

Recognizing residual aberrations

AberrationAppearance in the Ronchigram near focus
A1, two-fold astigmatismFringes stretched along one direction; the two sides of focus stretch in perpendicular directions
B2, axial comaThe uniform region is off-center or one-sided
A2, three-fold astigmatismContrast with three-fold symmetry around the center
C3, sphericalConcentric rings; the uniform region is small
Note. Corrections interact. Correcting B2 often changes A1 and C1, so always measure again after a change.

Step 4

Detectors

  1. Retract the Ronchigram camera if it shares the beam path with the STEM detectors.
  2. Select the camera length that gives the HAADF inner angle you need (at least three times the convergence semi-angle for Z-contrast imaging).
  3. For BF or ABF imaging, choose the camera length so that the detector covers the intended part of the bright-field disk.
  1. Start a fast scan on the specimen. Set the detector contrast and brightness so that the background is just above zero and the brightest features are not saturated. Check with the image histogram.

Result. The collection angles are known, and the image uses the detector's range without clipping.

Step 5

Final focus and stigmation

  1. On the crystal, at the zone axis, use a small field of view and a fast scan.
  2. Adjust focus (C1) for the sharpest columns.
  3. If the columns are elongated in one direction, and the direction changes by 90° through focus, correct with the probe stigmator (A1).
  4. If the columns show tails on one side, return to the Ronchigram and correct B2 (step 3).
  5. Set the pixel size, dwell time and scan rotation for acquisition.

Result. Columns are round and sharp, and the image does not change shape through a small focus range other than by blurring evenly.
